The road to a place

What the data say about getting onto the Doctorate in Clinical Psychology (DClinPsy): how many people get a place each year, how many tries trainees needed, and what the data cannot tell you. Each section has a label: Measured means counted, Approximate means it rests on one stated assumption, Scenario means "what if", not an estimate.

MeasuredClearing House figures, all applicants, 2015 to 2025.

Trainees who started about 2019 to 2021, on 30 courses. About 42% of trainees answered. This covers only people who got a place.

5. What the data suggest about the pattern

We tested a standard statistical model, set up before we looked at the result: everyone has their own fixed chance, and some people stop applying. It did not fit the survey. If it were right, about 41% of trainees would have got in first time. The real figure is 31%.

The survey pattern suggests that people applying again do a bit better, as a group, than first-timers. Fewer people make a second try than a first, yet the second try produced almost as many trainees (29% against 31%). No published table shows this directly, and it may be because of who chooses to reapply, not because trying again raises any one person's chance.

6. Scenario: what a yearly chance adds up to

ScenarioThis is not your chance. It shows the arithmetic only: if your chance were the same every year, and you applied every year, how likely would a place be after each try? Section 5 shows that real chances are not the same each try, so read it as a rough guide.

What these data cannot tell you

  • Your own chance. Experience, grades, interviews and which courses you choose are not in these data.
  • How many people never get a place. The survey asks only people who did.
  • Why first tries succeed less often. Several explanations fit equally well.
  • What happens next year. Places depend on NHS funding. Applicant numbers have grown faster than places since 2022.
